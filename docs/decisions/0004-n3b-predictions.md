## P4: the null redraw p-value

**P4.** If the null BSS tail (redraw p = 0.009, N3's null redraw check) was a chance lean in the
old outcome draw, the regenerated null's fixed-prediction redraw p-value is not below 0.05.

*Reading.* The procedure of N3's null redraw check (its record is not published), applied to the
N3b null_v1 run and nothing else changed:
- **Predictions held fixed.** `p_model` and `p_market` stay as the N3b review evaluated them, with
  no retraining per draw.
- **The draw.** One Bernoulli(`p_true_home`) per game, shared by the game's two contracts.
- **The statistic.** The 26.1-wf BSS, recomputed as the referee computes it:
  `bss(brier(y, p_model), brier(y, p_market))` over the review's evaluation predictions.
- **Size and seed.** 1,000 redraws, seed 20260926.
- **Checks, which must pass before a p-value is reported:**
  - the recomputed observed BSS equals the referee's `paired_game_bootstrap` value;
  - each game's two contracts agree on the recorded outcome and on `p_true`.
- **The p-value.** The share of redraws with a BSS at or above the observed one: the definition
  behind N3's 0.009.
- **Held** if that p-value is ≥ 0.05, that is, if at least 50 of the 1,000 redraws are at or above
  the observed BSS.
- (1 + count) / (1 + draws) is reported as well. With 1,000 draws it gives the same verdict: both
  are ≥ 0.05 exactly when the count is at least 50.

**What P4 can show (interpretation).** P4 reads its condition as the hypothesis being tested, and
judges the prediction on its consequent alone.
- If the old tail was chance, P4 still fails in about 5% of outcome draws. So a failure is not
  proof of leakage.
- A hold is consistent with chance, but it does not rule leakage out, for the reason N3's redraw
  check gave: redrawing outcomes cannot separate a lucky draw from predictions that carry
  outcome information.

The item 4 record gives each prediction's measured value next to its reading, and says held or not
held. It does not reinterpret a prediction after the numbers are known.
